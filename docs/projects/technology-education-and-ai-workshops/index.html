Technology Education Workshops

Workshops for kids and adults focused on practical troubleshooting, modern technical thinking, and responsible AI use.

Education 2022-Present Ongoing

One of the strongest throughlines in my work is technology education.

I do not just like using modern tools. I like helping other people understand them well enough to think clearly, adapt quickly, and use them with judgment.

That includes workshops and teaching environments with both kids and adults.

The goal is not hype. The goal is literacy.

In practice, that means helping people understand:

  • how AI tools fit into real workflows
  • how to think critically about modern technology instead of passively consuming it
  • how to troubleshoot systems instead of panicking when they fail
  • how software, hardware, and online systems shape everyday decisions
  • how to stay capable while tools keep changing underneath them

This work matters because the pace of change is real.

A lot of people can feel that technology is moving quickly without having a framework for how to respond to it. They know they are supposed to learn AI, adapt to new tools, and stay current, but nobody has shown them how to build judgment inside that environment.

That is where teaching becomes valuable.

For kids, the work is often about turning devices from entertainment objects into tools for reasoning, creativity, and problem solving.

For adults, the work is often about reducing intimidation, creating practical entry points, and showing that modern tools can be approached calmly and systematically.

In both cases, the core lesson is similar: technology should make people more capable, not more dependent and confused.

That perspective sits close to the rest of my work. Clear teaching, like clear websites and clear visual communication, helps people trust what they are looking at and understand what to do next.

Whether I am building websites, solving technical problems, improving operations, or teaching a workshop, I am usually doing the same thing underneath: helping people operate more clearly in systems that can otherwise feel opaque, noisy, or overwhelming.
