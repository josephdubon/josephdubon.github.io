Over the course of my freelance work, I have built and launched more than 100 websites and web applications for small businesses and organizations.

That work covered a wide spread of technical and practical requirements:

- React frontends
- Python-backed workflows
- WordPress builds and CMS implementations
- custom integrations
- maintenance and production troubleshooting

The important part was never just writing code. It was delivering something that worked in the context the client actually lived in.

That usually meant translating vague business requests into clear software decisions, choosing tools that matched the real complexity of the project, and shipping on timelines that did not leave room for overengineering.

This body of work shaped a lot of how I think about engineering:

- software should solve the real problem, not the imagined one
- handoff matters
- maintainability matters
- communication matters just as much as implementation

Freelance delivery also taught me to move comfortably between product thinking, debugging, implementation, and support. In a client environment, there is no luxury of pretending those are separate jobs.
