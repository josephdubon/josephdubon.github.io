Over the course of my freelance work, I built and launched more than 100 websites and web applications for small businesses and organizations.

That body of work remains one of the clearest foundations for how I work now. It trained me to move from ambiguity to delivery quickly, and to make technical decisions in contexts where time, clarity, trust, and presentation all mattered.

The work covered a wide spread of technical and practical requirements:

- React frontends
- Python-backed workflows
- WordPress builds and CMS implementations
- custom integrations
- maintenance and production troubleshooting

The important part was never just writing code. It was delivering something that worked in the environment the client actually lived in and helped them present themselves more professionally online.

That usually meant translating vague business requests into clear software decisions, choosing tools that matched the real complexity of the project, and shipping on timelines that did not leave room for overengineering.

That experience shaped a lot of how I think about client service now:

- solve the real problem, not the fashionable one
- handoff and documentation matter
- maintainability matters
- communication matters just as much as implementation
- presentation matters because people judge businesses through what they see first

Client delivery also taught me to move comfortably between product thinking, implementation, debugging, support, and stakeholder management. In practice, those are rarely separate jobs.
